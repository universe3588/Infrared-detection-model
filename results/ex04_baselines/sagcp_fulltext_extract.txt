Abstract— For infrared small-target detection, convolutional
neural network (CNN)-based methods have demonstrated
promising performance. However, due to the small size of the
targets, existing infrared small-target detection methods necessi-
tate intricate structures with intensive computation to maintain
distinctive features of targets in deep layers, which poses great
challenges for deployment on edge devices with constrained
resources. The current pruning methods predominantly focus
on the optimization of classification networks, with an emphasis
on semantic information. Nevertheless, the spatial details crucial
for infrared small-target detection are excessively pruned in
the shallow layers, resulting in a significant degradation of
detection performance. In this article, based on the sparse
distribution of small targets in infrared images, we propose a
sparsity-aware global channel pruning (SAGCP) framework to
optimize infrared small-target detection networks. Specifically,
sparse modeling is used to code the target region for the first time,
and sparse priors can be induced into the feature map for the
identification of redundant channels. Without the need for extra
structures or intricate criteria to identify redundant channels, our
pruning method can leverage the inherent properties of infrared
small targets to extract more robust features and obtain more
compact models. When SAGCP is applied to the existing infrared
small-target detection methods, the pruned network is superior in
model efficiency and detection performance. For example, when
applying our method to DNA-Net, the pruned model can achieve
a 72.34% reduction in parameters, and a 57.49% decrease in
floating point operations (FLOPs), but a 2.02% increase in
intersection over union (IoU).
Index Terms— Channel pruning, infrared small-target detec-
tion, sparse reconstruction.

In recent years, convolutional neural networks (CNNs)
have demonstrated robust performance in image classifica-
tion [1], [17] and object detection [48], [56] due to their strong
modeling capability. In contrast to general object detection,
infrared small targets typically occupy a limited spatial area
within the image due to the long imaging distance, leading to
restricted texture and shape information. Moreover, due to the
impact of atmospheric refraction/scattering and imaging noise,
infrared small targets are prone to be submerged in the clutters,
resulting in low contrast to the background. Consequently, the
general object detection methods [13], [55] cannot be directly
applied to infrared small-target detection.
To extract and maintain the features of small targets in
deep layers, the existing CNN-based SIRST detection meth-
ods employ various feature fusion strategies and attention
mechanisms. For example, Li et al. [27] introduced a dense
nested interaction module (DNIM) and a hierarchical channel
and spatial attention module (CSAM) to facilitate progres-
sive interaction between high- and low-level features through
iterative processes, thereby enhancing the features of small
targets. Guo et al. [14] developed a triple cross-layer fusion
module (TCFM) to exchange high-level semantics and fine-
grained details, which can preserve the subtle details of

Despite the promising performance of CNN-based methods
for infrared small-target detection, their computation-intensive
nature often compromises real-time inference efficiency for
detection performance. Consequently, there is an urgent need
to explore a lightweight network architecture for efficient
infrared small-target detection.
The existing model compression and acceleration tech-
niques, including parameter quantization [12], [51], model
pruning [4], [31], and knowledge distillation [47], [67],
have demonstrated significant efficiency improvement for
computation-intensive networks. Among these methods, struc-
tured pruning can effectively conserve storage resources and
enhance inference efficiency on standard hardware by directly
eliminating entire convolutional kernels or specific channels.
However, existing pruning methods are primarily tailored for
classification backbones, such as VGG [17] and ResNet [53].
In the field of infrared small-target detection, specific feature
extraction networks [24], [27], [54] are developed to maintain
the response of small targets in deep layers. Consequently,
directly applying the existing pruning methods to SIRST
detection is unsuitable due to the following reasons.
1) Prematurely Mispruning of Spatial Information for
SIRST Detection: Superior pruning methods for image
classification prioritize the preservation of filters con-
taining class-specific information. However, for infrared
small-target detection, the existing pruning methods may
lead to mispruning of position-sensitive filters, resulting
in significant performance degradation. This is because,
channels containing abundant spatial details in shallow
layers may be pruned prematurely, and such spatial
details are indispensable for the localization of infrared
small targets.
2) Isolation Between Pruning Criteria and Infrared
Images: The evaluation criteria of existing pruning meth-
ods utilize statistical measures inherent in the parameters
(e.g., ℓ1 norm [34] and geometric median [18]) to rank
the contributions of weights, activation, or gradients, and
identify “unimportant” neurons for pruning. However,
in infrared images, the target signal is characterized
by low energy, small size, and often low amplitude.
As a result, the existing evaluation criteria may lead to
the inadvertent removal of channels containing crucial
information about small targets. We hypothesize that this
is due to the fact that current channel pruning methods
evaluate significance in an isolation mannner from input
and lack a connection between pruning criteria and the
intrinsic properties of the data.
To address these issues, in this article, we propose a pruning
framework customized for SIRST detection networks, termed
sparsity-aware global channel pruning (SAGCP), which can
facilitate lossless compression to significantly improve model
efficiency while maintaining detection performance (as shown
in Fig. 1). Specifically, we introduce a sparse constraint layer
to utilize the sparse prior to reconstruct the input infrared

it can be employed for training redundant feature maps across

In summary, the contributions of this article can be summa-
rized as follows.
1) We develop a lossless pruning paradigm tailored for an
infrared small-target detection network, and propose an
SAGCP framework.
2) The proposed method does not require extra metrics
to identify redundant channels but directly utilizes the
sparsity of the input image, it can enhance the inference
efficiency but maintain the detection performance.
3) Experiments on different datasets demonstrate the supe-
rior performance. When applying SAGCP to existing
SIRST detection networks, the results validate the effec-
tiveness and universality of our method.

II. R ELATED WORKS
In this section, we first review the main research of infrared
small-target detection. Subsequently, we introduce the channel
pruning technology and its application for infrared small-target
detection.
A. Single-Frame Infrared Small-Target Detection
Traditional infrared small-target detection mainly includes
low-rank sparse decomposition [33], [60], [61] and local
contrast-based methods [15], [16], [59]. These methods rely on
assumptions about the target or background, assuming that the

these methods often lack the semantic distinction between the
targets and the background clutters in real scenes. Moreover,
the hand-made adjustment of hyperparameters poses chal-

scenarios.
Deep learning has been widely applied in target detection,
and the CNN-based method for infrared small-target detection
has yielded promising results. For example, Wang et al. [49]
decomposed infrared small-target detection into two sub-
tasks and employed a conditional generative adversarial
network (cGAN) to achieve a balance between miss detection
and false alarm. Dai et al. [5] developed a high-quality dataset
for SIRST detection, and proposed attentional local contrast
network (ALCNet) [6], which can effectively utilize the feature
learning ability of deep networks and the physical inter-
pretability of model-driven methods. Li et al. [27] designed
a dense nested interactive module (DNIM) and a cascaded
channel and spatial attention module (CSAM), to effec-
tively preserve detailed target information in deep layers.
Zhang et al. [63] proposed infrared shape network (ISNet)
which combined shape reconstruction with the detection of
small infrared targets. Ma et al. [37] utilized saliency label-
ing and multilayer feature fusion for infrared small-target
detection. Instead of utilizing complex feature fusion modules,
Kou et al. [25] integrated regular convolutions, depthwise
separable convolutions, atrous convolutions, and asymmet-
ric convolutions modules to develop a lightweight network
for infrared small-target detection. Li et al. [28] proposed an
MDAF module as the backbone network to enhance feature
reuse and preserve the features of infrared small target features
in deep layers. Despite the potential for robust detection per-
formance, complicated networks often entail a higher memory
allocation and increased computational resources.
B. Channel Pruning
Channel pruning reduces the number of parameters and
computational complexity of the model by removing channels
(or filters) with minimal impact on the final task, thereby
facilitating deployment on resource-constrained devices and
enhancing inference speed. The process of channel pruning
typically consists of three stages: first, pretraining to acquire
initial model weights; second, designing importance metrics
to prune channels with less saliency; and finally, fine-tuning
to recover the performance degradation resulting from prun-
ing. Li et al. [29] pruned filters by evaluating the weight
norm magnitude to eliminate ones with minimal impact on
output accuracy. He et al. [18] proposed a new importance
metric that moves away from the concept of “small norm-
unimportant” and identified substitutable filters by computing
their geometric medians. Chen et al. [4] designed a typical
three-stage global channel pruning method to identify unim-
portant channels to prune by integrating global and local
channel importance indicators. Sui et al. [44] proposed a filter
pruning method based on channel independence to evaluate
the significance of filters by quantifying the inter-feature map
correlation. Zhao et al. [65] developed a channel similarity
metric which quantifies differences between channels through

However, the existing pruning methods are mainly aimed
at image classification tasks and pay more attention to class-
sensitive features, often overlooking the feature correlation of
the interchannels. In contrast, infrared small-target detection
prioritizes feature aggregation and places greater emphasis
on location-sensitive features. As a result, direct pruning of
the network will lead to a significant degradation in the
performance of infrared small-target detection. Therefore, it is
essential to develop a pruning technique tailored for the SIRST
detection network based on the inherent characteristics of
infrared small targets.
C. Sparsity for Model Pruning
Sparse training for convolutional neural networks is typi-
cally implemented by imposing sparsity constraints, leveraging
unsaturated activation values, or employing specific sparsity
techniques. For example, Liu et al. [34] induced channel-level
sparsity through regularization on the scaling factor of the
batch-normalized layers. Wen et al. [50] employed lasso regu-
larization to directly learn the structure of compressible filters,
channels, and layer depths in the network during the training
stage. Huang and Wang [21] scaled the output of a specific
structure in a CNN, such as neurons, groups, or residual
blocks, by applying sparse regularization constraints on the
scaling factor. Lin et al. [32] aimed to achieve an optimal
tradeoff between minimizing loss and selecting filters by incor-
porating two different structural sparse regularization terms
into the original objective loss function of pruning. These
sparse training methods lack clear guidance, it is easy to fall
into local optimal solutions when they are directly applied to
infrared small-target detection.

C. SAGCP-Det
As shown in Fig. 4, we propose a lightweight infrared
small-target detection network. It takes an infrared image as
input and utilizes the sparse coding layer to map the infrared
image to sparse features. Subsequently, the sparse feature
extraction and feature fusion are performed to obtain the
final detection results. The trained model exhibits layer-wise
sparsity, wherein each layer of the network is characterized by
a significant number of channels with zero response values.
By directly removing unresponsive channels in a simpler way,
our method efficiently sparsifies the feature map and learns
robust features using interpretable layers in a more straightfor-
ward manner. The framework of the proposed SAGCP pruning
strategy is shown in Algorithm 1.

IRPruneDet is the first pruning method designed for the
SIRST detection network. It employs UNet-18 as the baseline
network and carries out wavelet structure-regularized soft
channel pruning, which decreases the number of parameters
and FLOPs. IRPruneDet utilizes ℓ1 norm to evaluate the
importance of channels after the wavelet transformation of the
convolutional layer. However, in infrared images, smaller norm
is not less important, thus, IRPruneDet may incur some per-
formance degradation. In contrast, the proposed SAGCP-Det
demonstrates superior detection performance (75.12 versus
77.52 in IoU) and further reduces the number of parameters
(0.18 M versus 0.06 M). Our SAGCP-Det performs sparse
reconstruction of infrared inputs directly, which can effectively
reduce the modeling loss for the targets.
The ROC performance curves of different methods on
NUAA-SIRST and IRSTD-1k are shown in Fig. 5. It can be
observed that our SAGCP-Det demonstrates superior perfor-
mance compared to other methods when dynamic thresholds
are employed. Specifically, the ROC curve of WSLCM cannot
be visualized within the selected coordinate interval on the
NUAA-SIRST dataset. For the more challenging IRSTD-1k
datasets characterized by strong clutter interference and signif-
icant target morphological variations, our SAGCP-Det exhibits
a more stable detection performance compared with other
methods.

detection.

V. C ONCLUSION
In this article, we propose an SAGCP framework to address
the significant storage and computational resource consump-
tion challenges in SIRST detection networks. Our pruning
method adaptively removes redundant channels from existing
infrared small-target detection networks by leveraging the
sparsity of the infrared images, and it can further improve
the model efficiency while maintaining the detection perfor-
mance. Experimental results on public datasets demonstrate
the effectiveness of our method. Compared to state-of-the-
art methods, our SAGCP-Det achieves comparable or even
better detection performance and has significant advantages in
terms of model size and computational cost. More importantly,
our pruning framework is compatible with existing SIRST
detection networks, and the pruned models can achieve compa-
rable detection results, with the model efficiency significantly
improved. This study represents an effective exploration of
the balance between detection performance and inference
